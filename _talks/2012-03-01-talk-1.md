Simplified stochastic models are widely used in Monte-Carlo based analyses of biochemical reaction networks to study frequency-resolved noise propagation and information transmission. The coherence spectra and mutual information rates are often estimated from reduced models under assumptions of timescale separation, and it is presumed that such reductions preserve information-theoretic measures of the underlying full systems. In this talk, using MonteCarlo methods and signal analysis, we show that this assumption does not hold for the coherence spectrum and therefore it does not hold for the lower bound of the mutual information rate either, even when the model reductions preserve lower statistics. Additionally, we illustrate how commonly employed reductions can distort frequency-dependent noise propagation and information flow.
