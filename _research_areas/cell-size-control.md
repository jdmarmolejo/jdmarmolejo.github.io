area: cell-size-control
---

How does a cell "know" how big it should be? Across organisms, populations of
proliferating cells maintain remarkably tight size distributions despite noisy
growth and division. This regularity points to feedback between growth and the
timing of division — captured by phenomenological *sizer*, *timer* and *adder*
strategies, and increasingly by molecular models of the underlying control.

My work here uses stochastic and coarse‑grained models to ask how size‑control
rules emerge from the coupling between cell growth, gene expression and the
division machinery, and how the choice of strategy shapes the fluctuations in
size, cycle duration and molecular content that we measure in single cells.
